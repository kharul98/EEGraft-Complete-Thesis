\chapter*{Abstract}
EEGraft studies whether EEG classifiers remain useful when recording time, decision thresholds and patient groups change. We built a gated dual-scale residual fusion decoder on a frozen CBraMod encoder and evaluated it alongside strong classical baselines. A five-minute SVM study documented changing labels and persistent errors. On a 13-person locked Alzheimer's-versus-control test, EEGraft achieved 70.24\% balanced accuracy and XGBoost 77.38\%. Without retraining, the models achieved 68.38\% and 58.57\%, respectively, on 57 external CAUEEG recordings labeled Normal or broad Dementia. Further experiments showed that changing XGBoost's threshold substantially changed apparent eye-condition consistency without changing its weights. Stable incorrect predictions were also observed, while age differences limited the interpretation of external results. Together, EEGraft contributes a documented decoder and a practical framework for evaluating accuracy, correctness, stability and cross-cohort behavior separately.
\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} EEG; dementia; Alzheimer's disease; CBraMod; dual-scale residual fusion; reliability; threshold sensitivity.
